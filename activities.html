Practice debates

Weekly · casual Lincoln-Douglas · open to all

Run by all three officers

The core of the club. We run casual Lincoln-Douglas rounds — one debater against one, on a values question rather than a policy plan — and the round exists to produce feedback, not a result.

You get notes on both halves of the skill: your one-on-one debating, and how you work with a partner when we run team formats. Nobody is keeping a record, and losing a practice round costs you nothing.

Topics come from the running list, and we take suggestions. If you want to argue something that is not on it, say so and we will run it.

Workshops

Cross-club · scheduled through the year

Run by all three officers

Sessions run jointly with other HTHS clubs, where the debating gets pointed at that club’s subject matter. You argue about markets with the finance people and about engineering decisions with ReconX, which is harder and more useful than arguing in the abstract.

Masterclasses

On request · one skill at a time

Request from any officer

Feeling stuck on one specific aspect of public speaking? Request a masterclass and we will build a workshop around exactly that skill.

This is deliberately narrow. Not “get better at speaking” but the one thing that is actually in your way — filler words, running out of air, freezing on rebuttal, reading off the page, going too fast when nervous, structuring a point under time pressure.

There is no minimum group size and no form. Tell an officer what the problem is, or post it in the Classroom, and we schedule one.

What it builds

The transferable half

Debate is a school subject dressed as an argument. Everything a round trains is something a class already grades you on.

Clarity

Convey ideas effectively

Say what you mean the first time, in an order the listener can follow, without needing a second run at it.

Presence

Converse effectively and with bravado

Commit to a position out loud. Most people lose an argument by sounding unsure of something they were right about.

Speed

Present fluently, argue quickly

Assemble a case in minutes instead of days — the exact thing an oral exam, an interview, or a cold-call question asks for.
