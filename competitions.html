Competitions, and what we can and cannot do

We prepare you to enter as an individual. Read the note below before you plan around anything on this page.

Read this first

We do not represent High Tech

The club does not send an official school team to outside competitions and does not represent High Technology High School at them. What follows are suggestions — competitions we think are worth entering and that we will actively help you prepare for as an individual.

That means practice rounds against officers, feedback on your cases, and walking you through the format. It does not mean registration, transport, chaperones, or entering under the school’s name. Those are yours to arrange.

ReconX × Debate Competition

October or November · joint with ReconX

Preparation: all officers

The main thing on the calendar this fall. A competition run jointly with ReconX, where the subject matter comes from their side and the format comes from ours.

We ran this in 2025, and the format and matchup document from that year is on the resources page. Read it before deciding whether to enter — it tells you how rounds were structured and who debated whom, which is most of what you want to know in advance.

HTHS debate competitions

In-school · through the year

Preparation: all officers

Competitions held inside High Tech. These are the easiest entry point if you have never competed: the format is the one you already practise weekly, the people are the ones you already debate, and there is nothing to travel to.

Dates go up in the Google Classroom as they are set.

Outside circuit competitions

Individual entry · you register

Preparation: all officers

If you find a tournament you want to enter, bring it to an officer. We will work the format with you, run practice rounds against it, and give feedback on your cases.

Registration, fees, and getting there are yours to handle — see the note at the top of this page. Start the conversation early rather than the week before; formats differ enough that a few sessions makes a real difference.

The formats guide is the place to start if you are not sure what a given tournament is actually asking for.
