What we argue about

The running list for practice rounds. You will be assigned a side, and it will regularly not be your own — that is the point.

TopicRoughly about
Drug legalization and decriminalizationWhere the line between the two sits, and what each is actually meant to fix.
Student loan forgivenessWho is owed what, and what forgiveness does to the people who come next.
Free and universal healthcareHealthcare as a right against healthcare as a market, and what each costs.
Flat tax vs. progressive taxFairness versus simplicity, and whether those are even in tension.
Gun controlRights, risk, and which restrictions survive contact with evidence.
Police reformWhat reform means concretely, and which version of it anyone is arguing for.
ElectionsHow they are run, who they represent, and what a better system would trade away.
And moreThe list is not fixed — see below.
Suggestions open

We are taking your topics

The list above is a starting point, not a syllabus. If you want to argue something else, propose it.

How

Propose one

Post it in the Google Classroom or tell an officer at a meeting. There is no form and no vote.

What works

A good topic

Something with a real case on both sides. If everyone in the room already agrees, there is no round in it.

Fair warning

You may argue against it

Sides are assigned. Suggesting a topic is not a way to guarantee arguing the side you believe.

Topics get run in casual Lincoln-Douglas rounds — values questions rather than policy plans. See how a practice debate works, or the formats guide for what Lincoln-Douglas asks for.

Joining

Come argue about one

No preparation expected. Turning up with no case and being handed a side is a normal first meeting.
